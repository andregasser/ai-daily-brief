Since yesterday, the control and scaling question has moved even further outside the model. Safety coordination hits a second hard boundary: antitrust law

Four paying users filed a lawsuit in the Northern District of California accusing Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and SpaceXAI of agreeing to slow AI development. The complaint ties its theory to the public debate following Dario Amodei's September 12 essay. Important: the alleged anticompetitive agreement has not been established by a court; AP reported that the companies had not immediately responded at publication time.

The IMF makes Europe's AI debate more measurable: productivity upside, but energy and dependency enter the equation

An IMF paper presented to EU finance ministers pairs the familiar estimate of roughly 1% cumulative productivity growth over five years with the constraints that can govern the next phase: uneven adoption, electricity grids, and dependence on foreign AI technology. Reuters also reports that roughly 60% of workers in advanced European economies are in highly AI-exposed jobs.

SEMICONDUCTORSCXMT · DRAM · CHINAReported

CXMT moves its fifth DRAM platform into mass production — important, but not an HBM breakthrough

CXMT has announced mass production of its fifth DRAM platform, Reuters reports. The company cites 11.95-nm feature spacing using quadruple patterning, at least 50% more gross dies per wafer than its prior generation, and new 24-Gbit LPDDR5X products. This is relevant industrialization evidence for the AI supply chain — but LPDDR5X is not HBM and does not automatically resolve China's previously documented HBM bottleneck.

What changed since yesterday?

The China-memory track had mostly been a shortage and pricing signal. Today adds credible counter-direction: domestic manufacturing is improving. That complicates the story rather than negating it.

Signal vs. Hype

Signal: greater domestic DRAM manufacturing capability and density. Hype brake: it does not establish HBM parity or immediate relief for AI-accelerator memory.

Qwen3.8-Omni-Flash turns multimodal production into a budgeting problem

Alibaba Cloud documents qwen3.8-omni-flash as accepting text, image, audio, and video input with text output. It supports function calling, web search, context caching, 113 audio languages/dialects, and multichannel audio. Its context window is 1M tokens; maximum input is 991,808 tokens without thinking or 983,616 with thinking, and maximum output is 131,072 tokens.

Why it matters

For real video/audio agents, “1M context” is only the ceiling. Sampling, resolution, media duration, cache hits, tool turns, and output reserve determine actual economics. Optimization shifts from model selection toward modality-aware context engineering.

Engineering Takeaway

Do not test accuracy alone. Log input tokens by modality, cache-hit rate, time-to-first-token, end-to-end latency, tool success, output tokens, and cost per successful task. For video, sampling density belongs in the eval provenance.

Signal vs. Hype

Signal: a broad multimodal API surface with tools and large context is directly testable now. Hype brake: capability support and context size do not establish real quality, latency, or cost on your workload; comparable independent end-to-end evidence is still missing.

🔬 Concept of the Day

MULTIMODAL TOKEN BUDGETING

A 1M context window is not a 1M-token free allowance

Intuition: with a text-only model, context engineering mostly means selecting and compressing text. With an omni model, several modalities compete for the same finite budget. A high-resolution image, dense video sampling, or long audio can consume context before the actual task, tool trajectory, and answer have enough room.

Why it matters technically: more media is not free. Alibaba documents pixel-dependent image tokenization for many vision models; video and audio add time and sampling as further dimensions. A smaller, selectively sampled media slice can therefore produce better end-to-end results than simply pushing everything into a 1M-token window.

Optimization levers: media pre-selection, adaptive frame sampling, resolution limits, VAD/audio segments, context caching, tool-based reacquisition, and explicit output reserve. Measure impact on task success, not just token reduction.

Connection to earlier concepts: this extends Active Perception. There the agent decides what deserves closer inspection; here that choice becomes a measurable cost, latency, and context budget.
